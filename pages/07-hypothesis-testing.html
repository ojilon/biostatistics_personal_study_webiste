Core Theory

Testing Claims About Populations

A null hypothesis ($H_0$) states no effect or no difference. The alternative ($H_1$) is what the researcher suspects. We compute a test statistic and compare it to a critical value (or use a p-value) to decide whether to reject $H_0$.

📌 Z-test Statistic

$$Z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$$

Reject $H_0$ if $|Z| > Z_{\alpha/2}$ (two-tailed) or if $p < \alpha$.

📌 Type I and Type II Errors

  • Type I ($\alpha$): Reject $H_0$ when it is true (false positive).
  • Type II ($\beta$): Fail to reject $H_0$ when $H_1$ is true (false negative).
  • Power $= 1 - \beta$. Larger $n$ and larger true effect increase power.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: A researcher obtains $p = 0.03$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, what decision is made? What if $\alpha = 0.01$?

Answer: At $\alpha = 0.05$, reject $H_0$ ($p < \alpha$). At $\alpha = 0.01$, fail to reject $H_0$ ($p > \alpha$). The same data can lead to different decisions depending on the chosen significance level.
