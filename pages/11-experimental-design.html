Core Theory

Principles of Experimental Design & ANOVA

Experimental Design ensures that research trials yield unbiased, valid, and precise estimates of treatment effects while minimizing experimental error. When comparing three or more treatment means simultaneously, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) partitions total dataset variation into explainable treatment effects and unexplained residual error.

📌 The Three Pillars of Design

1. Replication: Applying treatments to multiple experimental units to estimate error.

2. Randomization: Allocating treatments randomly to eliminate selection bias.

3. Local Control (Blocking): Grouping homogenous units to isolate environmental gradients.

Design Matrix

Classical Agricultural Experimental Designs

Design Layout Optimal Field Conditions Structural Features Primary Advantage
Completely Randomized Design (CRD) Homogeneous conditions (e.g., greenhouses, laboratories, poultry cages) Treatments assigned completely at random across all experimental units. Maximum degrees of freedom for error; simple layout and analysis.
Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) Heterogeneous fields with a single known gradient (e.g., soil fertility, slope) Experimental units grouped into blocks perpendicular to the gradient; treatments randomized within blocks. Isolates nuisance gradient variance from treatment variance, reducing experimental error.
Factorial Design Simultaneous evaluation of two or more independent treatment factors All combinations of factor levels are tested (e.g., 3 Nitrogen levels $\times$ 2 Crop Varieties). Evaluates main effects as well as **interaction effects** between treatment factors.

Post-Hoc Analysis

Post-Hoc Multiple Comparison Tests

1

Why Post-Hoc Tests Are Needed

A significant ANOVA $F$-test ($p < 0.05$) indicates that at least one treatment group mean differs from another, but does not pinpoint which specific groups differ.

2

Tukey's Honestly Significant Difference (HSD)

Controls the overall Family-Wise Error Rate ($\alpha = 0.05$) when performing pairwise comparisons between all possible group pairs.

3

Fisher's Least Significant Difference (LSD)

Calculates the minimum required difference between two means for significance. Highly sensitive but increases Type I error risk with many comparisons.

🌱 Field Practical Case Study: Namulonge Cassava Fertilizer Trial

An agronomist evaluates 3 fertilizer formulations ($k=3$) on cassava yield ($t/ha$) using 5 plot replicates per treatment ($N=15$ total plots):

  • ANOVA Output: $SS_{\text{Between}} = 142.8$, $df_{\text{Between}} = 2 \implies MS_{\text{Between}} = 71.4$
  • Residual Error: $SS_{\text{Within}} = 38.4$, $df_{\text{Within}} = 12 \implies MS_{\text{Within}} = 3.2$
  • F-Ratio: $$F = \frac{71.4}{3.2} = 22.31 \quad (p < 0.001)$$

Conclusion: $F = 22.31$ significantly exceeds the critical value $F_{0.05, 2, 12} = 3.89$. We reject $H_0$ and conclude that fertilizer type significantly alters cassava yield ($p < 0.001$). Post-hoc Tukey testing is conducted next to establish the top-performing formulation.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: An agricultural field slopes downward across a research site, causing moisture to accumulate at the bottom plots. What experimental design should be implemented, and why would using a Completely Randomized Design (CRD) be problematic?

Answer: A Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) must be used, with blocks arranged perpendicular to the moisture gradient. Using a CRD would allow moisture variability to inflate the experimental residual error term ($MS_{\text{Within}}$), drastically reducing statistical power and masking true treatment differences.
