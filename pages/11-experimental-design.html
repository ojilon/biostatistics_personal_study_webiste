Core Theory

Principles of Experimental Design & ANOVA

Good design yields unbiased estimates while minimizing error. ANOVA partitions total variation into treatment effects and residual error when comparing three or more means.

📌 Three Pillars of Design

1. Replication — multiple units per treatment to estimate error.

2. Randomization — remove selection bias.

3. Local control (blocking) — isolate environmental gradients.

Why post-hoc?

Significant ANOVA $F$ means at least one mean differs, but not which pairs.

2

Tukey HSD

Controls family-wise error rate for all pairwise comparisons.

3

Fisher LSD

Minimum significant difference; sensitive but raises Type I risk with many pairs.

🌱 Namulonge Cassava Fertilizer Trial

$k=3$ fertilizers, 5 replicates ($N=15$):

  • $SS_B = 142.8$, $df_B = 2$ → $MS_B = 71.4$
  • $SS_W = 38.4$, $df_W = 12$ → $MS_W = 3.2$
  • $$F = 71.4/3.2 = 22.31 \quad (p < 0.001)$$

Reject $H_0$; fertilizer type affects yield. Follow with Tukey post-hoc.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Field slopes so moisture pools at the bottom. Which design, and why is CRD problematic?

Answer: Use RCBD with blocks perpendicular to the moisture gradient. CRD would inflate $MS_W$ and mask true treatment differences.
