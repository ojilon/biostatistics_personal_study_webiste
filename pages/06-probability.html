Core Theory

Probability in Biological Systems

Biological events involve natural variability and uncertainty. Probability ($P$) quantifies the likelihood of a specific outcome occurring, expressed as a real number between $0$ (impossible event) and $1$ (certain event). Probability distributions provide the mathematical foundation for inferential statistics and hypothesis testing.

📌 Fundamental Axioms & Rules

Addition Rule (Mutually Exclusive Events):

$$P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$$

Multiplication Rule (Independent Events):

$$P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$$

Distribution Models

Key Biostatistical Probability Distributions

Distribution Model Data Type Parameters Probability Mass / Density Function Agricultural Application
Binomial Distribution Discrete (Binary outcomes) $n$ (trials), $p$ (success prob) $$P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1 - p)^{n - k}$$ Probability that $k$ out of $n=20$ beans germinate under drought stress.
Poisson Distribution Discrete (Rare events / area) $\lambda$ (mean rate) $$P(X = k) = \frac{\lambda^k \cdot e^{-\lambda}}{k!}$$ Number of fall armyworm egg masses counted per square meter of maize field.
Normal Distribution Continuous $\mu$ (mean), $\sigma$ (std dev) $$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\right)^2}$$ Modeling natural variation in cattle birth weight or crop canopy height.

Continuous Models

The Standard Normal Distribution ($Z$) & Empirical Rule

Any normally distributed continuous variable $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ can be transformed into the standard normal distribution $Z \sim N(0, 1)$ using the standard score transformation formula:

$$Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$$

68%

$$\mu \pm 1\sigma$$

Approximately 68.27% of all observations fall within 1 standard deviation of the population mean.

95%

$$\mu \pm 1.96\sigma$$

Exactly 95.00% of observations fall within $1.96$ standard deviations ($\approx 2\sigma$) of the mean.

99.7%

$$\mu \pm 3\sigma$$

Approximately 99.73% of observations fall within 3 standard deviations of the mean.

🥚 Field Practical Exercise: Egg Weight Probability Calculation

In a commercial flock, egg weights follow a normal distribution with mean $\mu = 60.0\text{ g}$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 2.5\text{ g}$. What is the probability that a randomly chosen egg weighs more than $65.0\text{ g}$?

  • Step 1 (Calculate Z-score): $$Z = \frac{65.0 - 60.0}{2.5} = \frac{5.0}{2.5} = 2.00$$
  • Step 2 (Consult Standard Normal Table): $P(Z < 2.00) = 0.9772$
  • Step 3 (Calculate Upper Tail Probability): $$P(X > 65.0) = 1 - P(Z < 2.00) = 1 - 0.9772 = 0.0228 \text{ (or } 2.28\%)$$

Conclusion: Only 2.28% of eggs produced by this flock will exceed $65.0\text{ grams}$.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: An agricultural field officer observes that parasitic ticks occur randomly on cattle in a paddock at an average rate of $\lambda = 2.0$ ticks per animal. Which probability distribution should be used to calculate the probability of finding 0 ticks on a randomly inspected cow?

Answer: The Poisson Distribution must be used because the data consists of rare, discrete count events occurring within a fixed sample unit (one cow). Using the formula $P(X = 0) = \frac{2.0^0 \cdot e^{-2.0}}{0!} = 1 \cdot e^{-2.0} \approx 0.1353$, there is a 13.53% chance of finding 0 ticks on a cow.
