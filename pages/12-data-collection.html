Core Theory

Data Integrity in the Field

Statistical validity is limited by the quality of primary data. Field collection needs protocols that minimize measurement error and observer bias.

📌 Observed Measurement Model

$$x_{\text{observed}} = x_{\text{true}} + \epsilon_{\text{systematic}} + \epsilon_{\text{random}}$$

$\epsilon_{\text{systematic}}$ reduces accuracy; $\epsilon_{\text{random}}$ reduces precision.

Quality Control

Managing Error in Biological Sampling

1

Systematic error (bias)

Consistent shift in one direction (uncalibrated scale, biased frame). Remedy: daily zero-point calibration.

2

Random variation

Unpredictable fluctuation (environment, technique). Remedy: larger $n$, standardized timing.

3

Observer bias

Evaluator scores treated groups higher. Remedy: double-blind coding.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: An uncalibrated glucometer always reads $0.8$ mmol/L high. Does that hurt accuracy, precision, or both?

Answer: Systematic bias hurts accuracy. Precision can stay high if repeats give the same offset, but every value is shifted upward.
