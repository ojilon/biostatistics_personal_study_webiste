Core Theory

The Center of Biological Data

A Measure of Central Tendency is a single value that attempts to describe a set of data by identifying the central position within that data set. Selecting the appropriate metric depends on scale of measurement, presence of outliers, and distribution symmetry.

📌 Mathematical Formulas

Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$):

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i}{n}$$

Weighted Mean ($\bar{x}_w$):

$$\bar{x}_w = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} (w_i \cdot x_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} w_i}$$

🐄 Agricultural Case Study: Milk Yield Outlier Impact

Consider 5 cows yielding daily milk (liters):

Baseline: 12, 14, 15, 16, 18 $\rightarrow$ $\bar{x} = 15.0$ L, Median $= 15.0$ L

With outlier: 12, 14, 15, 16, 48 $\rightarrow$ $\bar{x} = 21.0$ L, Median $= 15.0$ L

A single extreme cow pulls the mean up 40%; the median stays stable.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Cassava foliage intake for 9 goats: 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.5, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.1, 4.5. Which central tendency measure is most representative, and why?

Answer: The Median ($1.6$ kg). The outlier $4.5$ inflates the mean to $1.96$ kg, which exceeds 7 of 9 animals. The median is robust.
