Core Theory

The Test Selection Framework

Parametric tests assume normality and equal variance and offer higher power when assumptions hold. Non-parametric tests use ranks or counts and are robust to non-normality, small samples, and ordinal data.

📌 Parametric Assumptions Checklist

1. Continuous interval or ratio scale.

2. Normality of the population distribution.

3. Homogeneity of variance ($\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$).

4. Independence of observations.

Diagnostic Tools

Testing Parametric Assumptions

1

Shapiro-Wilk Test

$$p > 0.05 \implies \text{normal}$$

$$p \le 0.05 \implies \text{non-normal}$$

2

Levene's Test

$$p > 0.05 \implies \text{equal variances}$$

$$p \le 0.05 \implies \text{use Welch's test}$$

3

Q-Q Plot

Observed quantiles vs expected normal quantiles; points should follow a 45° line.

🐛 Crop Disease Severity Ratings

Ordinal severity scores ($1$–$5$) across two practices: use Mann-Whitney $U$ (not a $t$-test). Example: $U = 22.5$, $p = 0.014$ → significant difference in median ranks.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Milk yields ($N=15$), Shapiro-Wilk $p = 0.003$. Independent $t$-test or Mann-Whitney $U$?

Answer: Mann-Whitney $U$. Normality is rejected ($p = 0.003 < 0.05$) in a small sample, so parametric $t$-tests are unreliable.
