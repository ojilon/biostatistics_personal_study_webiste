Core Theory

Population vs. Sample

A Population ($N$) comprises the complete set of all individual items under study, while a Sample ($n$) is a representative subset chosen to infer properties about the population.

📌 Key Principle

Sampling Error ($|\bar{x} - \mu|$) decreases as sample size ($n$) increases and when stratification accounts for field heterogeneity.

🚜 Practical Field Protocol: Stratified Sampling

When sampling a field with environmental gradients:

  • Divide into uniform zones (strata).
  • Assign plot numbers within each stratum.
  • Draw random samples independently from each stratum (proportional allocation).
  • Combine stratum means weighted by area for an unbiased estimate.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Why does Stratified Random Sampling yield lower sampling error than SRS when a field has a strong soil fertility gradient?

Answer: Stratified sampling forces units from both high and low fertility zones. SRS risks under- or over-sampling high-yield areas by chance, increasing variance and sampling error.
