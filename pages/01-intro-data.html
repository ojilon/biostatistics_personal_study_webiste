Core Theory

What is Biological Data?

In biostatistics, data consists of values or attributes recorded from biological entities—such as crop yields, animal body mass, pathogen incidence, or physiological rates. Accurate statistical analysis depends entirely on identifying the correct data type before choosing statistical tests.

📌 Key Takeaway

The choice of descriptive statistics (mean vs. median) and hypothesis tests ($t$-test vs. Chi-square) is determined by whether your variable is qualitative or quantitative.

Measurement Framework

The Four Levels of Measurement

1

Nominal Scale

Labels only. Operations: counting ($N$), mode.

Example: Cattle breed (Ankole, Friesian, Zebu).

2

Ordinal Scale

Ranks with unequal intervals. Operations: median, percentiles.

Example: Soil fertility (Low, Medium, High).

3

Interval Scale

Equal intervals, no true zero.

Example: Soil temperature (°C).

4

Ratio Scale

Equal intervals and true zero.

Example: Root dry weight (g), canopy height (cm).

🌱 Field Practical Exercise

NARO bean trial parameters: Leaf area ($cm^2$), Resistance status (Resistant / Susceptible), Pod count, Flower color. Identify the scale of each before analysis.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Why can you calculate the mean for leaf area (ratio) but not for disease severity grade (ordinal)?

Answer: Ordinal intervals are not uniform, so addition and division are invalid. Use the median or mode for ordinal data.
