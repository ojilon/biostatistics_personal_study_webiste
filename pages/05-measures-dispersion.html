Core Theory

Why Measure Dispersion?

Two herds can share the same mean milk yield yet differ widely in spread. Dispersion metrics quantify scatter around the center.

📌 Variance & SD

$$s^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1} \qquad s = \sqrt{s^2}$$

🌽 Coefficient of Variation

Egg weights: $\bar{x}=60.5$, $s=2.8$ → $CV=4.63\%$. Maize yield: $\bar{x}=14.8$, $s=2.2$ → $CV=14.86\%$. Maize has greater relative variability despite a smaller $s$.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Why can you not compare $s=35$ kg (body weight) directly with $s=0.8$ g/dL (serum protein) to say which trait is more variable?

Answer: Different units and scales. Use the unitless Coefficient of Variation (CV%) to compare relative variability.
