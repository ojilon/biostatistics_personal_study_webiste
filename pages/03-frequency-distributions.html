Core Theory

Population vs. Sample

A Population ($N$) comprises the complete set of all individual items or biological entities under study, while a Sample ($n$) is a representative subset chosen to infer properties about the population. Proper sampling designs prevent selection bias and ensure valid inferential statistics.

📌 Key Principle

Sampling Error ($|\bar{x} - \mu|$) measures the discrepancy between a sample estimate and the true population parameter. It decreases as sample size ($n$) increases and when stratification accounts for field heterogeneity.

Probability Sampling Framework

Probability Sampling Techniques

Sampling Design How It Works Key Advantage Biological / Agricultural Application
Simple Random (SRS) Every unit in population has an equal, independent chance of selection. Completely unbiased, simple statistical setup. Selecting 20 random chickens from a homogeneous indoor flock.
Stratified Random Population divided into homogeneous strata (subgroups); random samples taken from each. Ensures representation across gradients (e.g., soil fertility, slope). Sampling cassava yields across North (High Fertility) vs South (Low Fertility) plots.
Systematic Selects every $k^{\text{th}}$ item ($k = N/n$) after a random start point. Easy and efficient for field layout and line transects. Sampling pest counts on every $5^{\text{th}}$ maize plant along a crop row.
Cluster Population grouped into clusters; entire clusters randomly chosen for measuring. Cost-effective for widely dispersed field geography. Selecting 3 entire sub-counties in Mukono district to survey cattle tick burden.

🚜 Practical Field Protocol: Stratified Sampling

When sampling an agricultural field with environmental gradients (such as a slope leading toward a wetland):

  • Divide the field into distinct uniform zones (Strata: Upper Slope, Mid Slope, Valley Floor).
  • Assign plot numbers within each stratum.
  • Use proportional allocation to draw random samples independently from each stratum.
  • Combine stratum means weighted by area size to obtain an unbiased field yield estimate.

💡 Knowledge Check

Question: Why does Stratified Random Sampling yield lower sampling error than Simple Random Sampling when a field exhibits a strong soil fertility gradient?

Answer: Stratified random sampling accounts for variation between strata by forcing sample units to be drawn proportionally from both high and low fertility zones. Simple random sampling risks under-sampling or over-sampling high-yield areas purely by chance, resulting in higher variance and sampling error.
